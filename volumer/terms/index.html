2. Hearing safety

Some features play sound louder than your phone allows on its own:

  • Boost – Media above the device maximum (up to about +10 dB).
  • Volume layer above 0 % – everything that plays as media gets louder still (up to about +10 dB, together with the boost up to the limit of the audio effect).

Before you use them the first time, the app asks you to confirm a hearing warning. Please:

  • use them only briefly and only at a level that is comfortable;
  • take particular care with headphones and earbuds, where loud sound is close to your ears – start low and raise the volume step by step;
  • stop and lower the volume if you feel discomfort, ringing in your ears or muffled hearing, and see a doctor if it doesn't go away;
  • don't let children use the boost or a louder volume layer without supervision;
  • keep in mind that your phone's own hearing protections (such as the headphone volume warning) limit the system volume, not sound the app adds on top of it.

The World Health Organization recommends keeping listening levels and time limited; as a rule of thumb, the louder it is, the shorter you should listen. You use the boost and a louder volume layer at your own risk.

Speakers can also be damaged by very loud sound. The app limits peaks, but it can't know the limits of every speaker or headphones.

3. Volumes, alarms and notifications

The quiet zone, profiles, automation and the sleep timer can make sounds very quiet or silent. It is your responsibility to check that you can still hear calls, alarms, notifications and other sounds you need, especially before you rely on an alarm.

The app's protection features (volume limits, locks) are a convenience, not a medical or certified hearing-protection device.

What the app can do depends on your phone and its operating system. Some features may not work on some devices, and a system or vendor update may change them.
